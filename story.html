How it works

Where the trucks come from

Facebook Marketplace caps any search at 500 miles, so the engine tiles five overlapping searches — Lincoln, Denver, Dallas, Memphis, Chicago — to cover a ~1,000-mile ring, then deduplicates by listing ID.

How a truck gets priced

Every asking price is compared to a private-party value triangulated from Kelley Blue Book fair-purchase data, Edmunds appraisals by trim, and the median asking price of comparable trucks in the same dataset — adjusted about nine cents per mile against typical mileage.

Rebuilt titles get a 25% haircut. Anything 15%+ under book is flagged, then verified by hand: mileage, title status, seller account age.

Why most "deals" die on inspection

Of 31 listings that looked 10%+ under market, most collapsed once the detail page was opened: six-figure odometers, branded titles, or sellers who joined Facebook last month. Three survived. That ratio — 31 to 3 — is the whole reason to run the numbers before falling in love with a truck.
